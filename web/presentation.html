Motivation & Background

Why PQC Signatures?

⚡
Quantum Threat to Classical Crypto

Shor's algorithm (FOCS 1994) efficiently factors integers and computes discrete logs — breaking RSA, DSA, and ECDSA on any sufficiently large quantum computer. "Harvest now, decrypt later" makes migration urgent today.

🏛️
NIST Standardization Timeline

2024: NIST standardized ML-DSA (Dilithium), FN-DSA (FALCON), SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+). Then opened a second call for diversity. HAWK advanced to Round 3 — May 2026 (NIST IR 8610).

🔀
Where HAWK Fits

HAWK's security rests on omSVP and smLIP — independent of the SIS/LWE problems underlying ML-DSA and FN-DSA. It diversifies the algebraic footprint of the PQC portfolio.

🔧
Design Goals

Speed: fast on ARM Cortex-M4. Compactness: ≤14 KiB RAM for HAWK-512. Constraint-friendly: zero floating-point arithmetic — viable on MCUs without an FPU.

Scheme Overview

Scheme Overview

High-Level Idea: Hash-and-Sign over Rings

🔑 Private Key (secret basis)

Unimodular matrix B ∈ GL₂(Rₙ), rows (f,F) and (g,G), satisfying fG − gF = 1. Stored compactly as seed + parities of F,G mod 2 + hash of Q.

🗝️ Public Key (Gram matrix)

Q = B⋆B, determined entirely by q₀₀ and q₀₁. HAWK-512: 1,024 bytes. HAWK-1024: 2,440 bytes.

✍️ Signing Flow (high level)
①Hash m via SHAKE-256 twice → binary target h ∈ R²ₙ
②Use secret B to sample short w in coset 2R²ₙ + Bh
③Apply SYM-BREAK; output only s₁ = (h₁ − w₁)/2 + salt
④HAWK-512 signature: 555 bytes
✅ Verification

Reconstruct s₀ from public key via 32-bit fixed-point arithmetic. Check ‖w‖²_Q ≤ 4σ²_verify · 2n. All public data — no timing side channels.

Background & Preliminaries

Rings, Distributions & NTRU

🔢
Cyclotomic Ring

Rₙ = ℤ[X]/(Xⁿ+1), n ∈ {256, 512, 1024}. Negacyclic structure enables efficient NTT-based polynomial multiplication.

📊
Centred Binomial (KeyGen)

Bin(η): XOR pairs of uniform bits — constant-time, zero FPU. Var = η/2, mean 0. Security parameter η varies per parameter set.

🎲
Discrete Gaussian (Sign)

D_{2ℤ+c, σ}, c ∈ {0,1}. Two fixed precomputed lookup tables — no key-dependent or message-dependent timing whatsoever.

📐
Rényi Divergence

Bridges ideal vs. finite-precision Gaussian samplers in the security proof. Satisfies data processing inequality & probability preservation bounds.

🧮
NTRU Equation

KeyGen solves fG − gF = 1 (mod Xⁿ+1) via NTRUSOLVE. Dominant cost: ~8.43×10⁶ cycles for HAWK-512 on x86 AVX2.

🔐
Hardness Assumptions

omSVP — forgery resistance. smLIP — key recovery resistance. Both independent of SIS/LWE.

Algorithms

Algorithms

KeyGen  ·  Sign  ·  Verify

🔑 HAWK-KEYGEN
① Sample random kgseed
② Derive f, g ∼ Bin(η) deterministically
③ NTRUSOLVE → find F, G
④ Reduce F, G; build B; compute Q = B⋆B
⑤ sk = (seed, F mod 2, G mod 2, h_pub)
⑥ pk = Q — HAWK-512: 1,024 B
✍️ HAWK-SIGN
① Regen f, g from seed
② M = SHAKE-256(m ‖ h_pub); random salt
③ h = SHAKE-256(M ‖ salt) ∈ R²ₙ
④ Sample x ∼ D_{2ℤ²ⁿ+t, σ}
⑤ w = B⁻¹x; apply SYM-BREAK
⑥ Output s₁ only — 555 B (HAWK-512)
✅ HAWK-VERIFY
① Recompute h as in Sign
② w₁ = h₁ − 2s₁; check SYM-BREAK
③ Reconstruct s₀ via 32-bit fixed-point
④ w₀ = h₀ − 2s₀; form w
⑤ Check ‖w‖²_Q ≤ 4σ²_v · 2n
⑥ All public data — no timing channels

🔁 Restart probability: ≈ 1/200,000 (HAWK-512), ≈ 1/400,000 (HAWK-1024) — essentially deterministic throughput. Fixed Gaussian tables are load-bearing for the Rényi divergence security argument.

Security & Cryptanalysis

Security Assumptions & Attack Models

omSVP — Forgery Resistance

One-more approx. SVP: given poly-many short vectors, find one more. SUF-CMA reduces to omSVP in the qROM. Parameters calibrated via independent BKZ experiments — not from the reduction alone (a known methodological gap).

smLIP — Key Recovery Resistance

Recovering B from Q = rank-2 smLIP over a CM-field. Poly-time algorithm found for totally real fields (2024), but CM-field case remains open — confirmed by 4 major papers in 2024–25.

⚡ Side-Channel Notes

Fixed lookup tables → isochronous execution. 2024 power-analysis attack on reference impl.; countermeasures proposed. Provable Gaussian sampler masking remains open.

✅ HAWK Advantages

No FPU; lowest RAM for signing; cryptographic diversity (omSVP/smLIP ≠ SIS/LWE); SUF-CMA in qROM; ideal for IoT and embedded MCUs without FPU.

⚠️ HAWK Tradeoffs

No FIPS designation yet; omSVP/smLIP are newer and have less accumulated scrutiny; Gaussian sampler masking unsolved; use ML-DSA/FN-DSA now for regulated environments.

Current Relevance & Practical Status

Standardization Status & Deployment Guidance

📅 NIST Round 3 — May 2026

9 of 14 candidates advanced (NIST IR 8610). HAWK is the only lattice-based scheme remaining. Revised specs due August 14, 2026. Round 3 ≈ 2 more years.

🎯 Deployment Today

Regulated environments: use ML-DSA or FN-DSA now. Treat HAWK as a future cryptographic agility candidate. Implementation: hawk-sign.info (Python + C).

⚙️ Hardware Suitability

HAWK-512: ≤14 KiB RAM, floating-point free, <0.1 ms on desktop. Uniquely attractive for IoT and MCUs without FPU — niche currently unserved by existing FIPS standards.

🔬 Quantum Resistance

Security does not rely on factoring or discrete log — immune to Shor's algorithm. No known quantum speedup comparable to Shor's for BKZ on these lattice problems.

Conclusion

Key Takeaways & Open Problems

Key Takeaways
🦅

HAWK provides cryptographic diversity — omSVP & smLIP independent of SIS/LWE; algebraic footprint diversification for the PQC portfolio.

⚙️

Hardware-friendly: zero FPU, ≤14 KiB RAM, isochronous execution. Ideal for IoT/embedded MCUs — fills a niche FALCON cannot.

🏅

Proves SUF-CMA in the qROM — stronger than EUF-CMA, protecting against replay and double-spend attacks in real protocols.

Open Problems
❓

smLIP over CM-fields — 4 major papers in 2024–25; CM case still open but actively studied. Outcome determines HAWK's long-term viability.

⚡

Provable Gaussian sampler masking against power-analysis attacks — demonstrated attacks exist; provable defense remains unsolved.

🔬

HAWK-256 challenge set — breaking it would constitute significant scientific progress; accessible cryptanalytic target for the community.
